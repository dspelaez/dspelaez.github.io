## Project Overview

Submesoscale features such as fronts, filaments and small eddies, with sizes from a few hundred metres to about ten kilometres, play a key role in the exchange of heat, carbon and nutrients between the surface and the ocean interior. They are also too small and too fast to be resolved by most satellite missions. This project, carried out at the Laboratoire d'Océanographie Physique et Spatiale (LOPS) and funded by CNRS and CNES, uses a tandem configuration of Sentinel-2 optical satellites to image these currents directly from space.

## Objectives

- Develop a method to retrieve surface currents from pairs of Sentinel-2 images acquired in tandem
- Track the propagation of surface waves between the two acquisitions
- Map submesoscale current structures at kilometre resolution
- Validate the retrieved currents against in-situ observations and other remote sensing products

## Methodology

The project combines:
- **Sentinel-2 MSI imagery**: high-resolution optical images of the sea surface where individual waves are visible
- **Tandem acquisitions**: two satellites imaging the same area a short time apart, used to measure how the waves move
- **Wave dispersion analysis**: deviations from the theoretical wave dispersion relation used to infer the underlying current
- **Validation**: comparison with drifters, HF radar and other satellite observations

## Significance

This work contributes to:
- New observations of submesoscale ocean dynamics from space
- Better understanding of wave-current interactions
- Preparation and validation of future satellite missions dedicated to ocean surface currents
